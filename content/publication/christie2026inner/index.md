summary: Inner product regularization encourages locally consistent responses to state perturbations in imitation learning.
abstract: Inner product regularization (InneR) encourages imitation policies to respond consistently to the same perturbation at nearby states. It groups states with aligned policy-induced inner products and regularizes their average so that the policies share directions of insensitivity. The method supports deterministic and stochastic policies, improving robustness to irrelevant state dimensions and distribution shifts. Experiments include simulated systems and diffusion policies for real-world robot manipulation.
